# BE59 — Marine Domain Adaptation Assessment

## 1. Objective

Assess whether the synthetic-trained Marine FNO V2 model is likely to generalize to real Mombasa marine conditions, identify the principal differences between its training domain and available historical data, and determine whether domain adaptation is currently justified.

This assessment builds on:

- BE52 — Marine data audit
- BE57 — Independent real-world marine model validation
- BE58 — Historical hindcast feasibility
- BE55 — Mombasa bathymetry datasets
- BE56 — Simulated shallow-water training data
- BE57 — Marine FNO V2 training and evaluation

The assessment is evidence-based. Differences between datasets are not automatically model errors, and synthetic evaluation results are not treated as real-world forecast skill.

## 2. Executive summary

Marine FNO V2 (`fno-0.5.0`) was trained using simulated shallow-water scenarios over a GEBCO-derived Mombasa domain. It performs better than persistence and a simplified harmonic baseline on its held-out synthetic test scenarios.

However, the available evidence does not establish that it can accurately forecast real Mombasa conditions.

The investigation identified several domain differences:

1. Historical wind conditions in the available ERA5 sample extend beyond the wind speeds represented in synthetic training.
2. Synthetic tidal forcing uses a simplified sinusoidal eastern boundary, whereas observed sea-level variability is more complex.
3. Synthetic simulations start from zero water-level anomaly and zero currents, unlike historical marine conditions.
4. The shallow coastal bathymetry has a different GEBCO source composition from the deeper domain, with substantial optical-source and predicted-altimetry contributions.
5. Available historical current data do not provide the hourly, spatially resolved observations required for independent validation of the model's current forecasts.

The shallow-water solver's mass-balance accounting is internally consistent to machine precision in the tested scenarios. Wind sensitivity is concentrated in shallow coastal areas. These findings are useful diagnostics, but neither establishes independent physical accuracy.

**Decision:** Domain adaptation is a justified research direction, but immediate fine-tuning or retraining is not justified. The current evidence is insufficient to demonstrate that adaptation would improve real-world forecast skill.

## 3. Model and synthetic training domain

### 3.1 Model configuration

The assessed model is Marine FNO V2:

| Property | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Model version | `fno-0.5.0` |
| Dataset version | V2 |
| Input channels | 13 |
| Output variables | Water level, eastward current, northward current |
| Forecast horizon | Six hourly steps |
| Output shape | `[batch, 6, 3, ny, nx]` |
| Training scenarios | 12 |
| Validation scenarios | 3 |
| Test scenarios | 3 |
| Training windows | 84 |
| Validation windows | 21 |
| Test windows | 21 |
| Training epochs | 20 |
| Best epoch | 18 |
| Checkpoint | `artifacts/marine_fno_v2.pt` |

The model accepts spatial initial fields, but the training scenarios are generated by the same simplified shallow-water simulation system.

### 3.2 Synthetic forcing

The 18 generated scenarios combine six wind profiles with three tidal profiles.

Wind profiles include calm conditions and selected constant cardinal-direction winds, with the strongest configured wind reaching 5 m/s.

Tidal amplitudes are 0.35 m, 0.5 m and 0.8 m. Each amplitude is paired with a particular phase, meaning amplitude and phase are not independently varied across all combinations.

The scenarios share the same computational domain, bathymetry, water mask and solver configuration.

The simulations begin from zero water-level anomaly and zero currents. Later training windows use evolved states from those simulations, but these are still synthetic states.

### 3.3 Synthetic evaluation

On the held-out synthetic test scenarios, pooled MAE was:

| Variable | FNO V2 | Persistence | Harmonic baseline |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Water level (m) | 0.028346 | 0.333792 | 0.309838 |
| Eastward current (m/s) | 0.001542 | 0.004332 | 0.004332 |
| Northward current (m/s) | 0.001025 | 0.002062 | 0.002062 |

These results demonstrate that the model learned useful predictive behaviour within the synthetic scenario distribution.

They do not demonstrate real-world skill. The 21 test windows originate from only three held-out scenarios, and the reported metrics are pooled across scenarios, forecast lead times and water cells.

## 4. Evidence of domain differences

### 4.1 Wind forcing

The available ERA5 sample covers 1–3 October 2022.

Its wind-speed distribution was:

| Statistic | Wind speed |
|---|---:|
| 5th percentile | 2.27 m/s |
| Median | 5.84 m/s |
| 95th percentile | 8.11 m/s |
| Maximum | 9.69 m/s |

The strongest synthetic training wind is 5 m/s.

This sample therefore contains wind conditions outside the strongest configured synthetic scenario. It also contains directional variability that is not represented by a small set of constant cardinal-direction profiles.

Important limitations:

- The sample covers only three days and should not be treated as a climatology.
- Only two ERA5 grid points fall inside the FNO domain.
- Regridding these data cannot create additional independent spatial information.

**Assessment:** The synthetic wind-forcing range and spatial representation are limited relative to the available historical sample. Broader and more varied wind scenarios are a reasonable training-data improvement.

### 4.2 Tidal and sea-level forcing

Synthetic scenarios use a single sinusoidal tidal signal imposed at the eastern boundary. The other boundaries are reflective.

The available historical evidence is more complex:

- UHSLC provides an independent hourly Mombasa gauge record.
- BE52 harmonic analysis identified substantial M2 and S2 constituents.
- The estimated M2 amplitude was approximately 1.074 m, and the S2 amplitude approximately 0.514 m.
- Their estimated spring-range contribution was approximately 3.177 m.

The synthetic boundary amplitudes range from 0.35 m to 0.8 m. These are not directly equivalent to the gauge's constituent amplitudes or local water-level range because boundary location, phase, datum and spatial response differ.

The Copernicus merged hourly tide product also showed promising agreement with the demeaned gauge series in the October 2022 sample, with a correlation of approximately 0.963 and RMSE of approximately 0.236 m.

However, absolute vertical datum compatibility remains unresolved. The Copernicus `ocean_tide`, `sea_surface_height` and `total_sea_level` variables must not be treated as interchangeable.

**Assessment:** The single-sinusoid boundary is a substantial simplification. A more defensible future scenario generator should represent multiple tidal constituents and investigate spatially and temporally varying boundary conditions, with datum consistency explicitly handled.

### 4.3 Initial conditions

Synthetic simulations begin with:

- Zero water-level anomaly.
- Zero eastward current.
- Zero northward current.

The FNO accepts spatial initial conditions, but its training states are generated from this simplified simulation setup.

Historical conditions may contain residual sea-level anomalies, non-zero currents and spatial gradients.

The available GLORYS12V1 sample provides regional ocean-state context, but it is daily mean data. It cannot reconstruct the hourly tidal and current evolution required for a directly comparable hourly hindcast.

**Assessment:** Historical initial-state distributions are not represented adequately by the existing synthetic scenario design. Future training scenarios should include varied, physically consistent initial states.

### 4.4 Bathymetry source and shallow-water uncertainty

The model domain uses GEBCO 2026 bathymetry.

The original source grid has approximately 15-arc-second spatial resolution. It was interpolated onto a projected 250 m grid. The finer target spacing does not create independent 250 m seabed measurements.

The source metadata indicates:

- Vertical reference: height above mean sea level.
- No vertical datum correction applied.
- No source accuracy claim.
- Source resolution approximately 15 arc-seconds.

The projected domain contains 15,562 covered underwater cells.

The source-type distribution varies with depth:

| GEBCO TID | Meaning | Entire domain | 0–10 m | 10–20 m |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | Singlebeam | 15.0% | 0.0% | 0.4% |
| 14 | ENC sounding | 4.1% | 1.7% | 8.3% |
| 16 | Optical | 8.2% | 64.4% | 33.6% |
| 40 | Predicted altimetry | 66.7% | 19.4% | 43.4% |
| 44 | Bathymetric sounding | 6.1% | 14.5% | 14.3% |

TID classifications describe source type, not quantitative uncertainty or guaranteed accuracy.

In particular, optical-source classification does not by itself establish that a depth is inaccurate. Independent depth observations would be needed to quantify errors.

The investigation traced a cluster of 1 m bathymetry values near the strongest wind-response hotspot to the original GEBCO source. The nearest source cell contains elevation -1 m and TID 16. The preprocessing pipeline does not introduce a 1 m minimum-depth floor.

**Assessment:** Shallow-water bathymetry warrants particular caution because the shallow region has a different source composition from the deeper domain. Bathymetric uncertainty should be investigated before interpreting local shallow-water current predictions as reliable. The current dataset is not established as navigation-grade.

### 4.5 Spatial and temporal resolution of historical products

The available historical products have complementary but important limitations:

| Dataset | Useful information | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| ERA5 | Hourly historical wind forcing | Coarse spatial resolution; short sample assessed |
| UHSLC gauge | Independent hourly water-level observations | One measurement location; no spatial current field |
| Copernicus merged tide | Candidate hourly tidal/sea-level information | Datum compatibility and variable interpretation require care |
| GLORYS12V1 | Regional currents and sea-level context | Daily means; coarse relative to the FNO grid |
| GEBCO 2026 | Domain bathymetry | Source-resolution and source-quality limitations |

GLORYS12V1 maps over much of the FNO domain, but the mapping is based on only 18 distinct source grid cells. Many FNO cells therefore inherit information from the same coarse source cell.

These products cannot simply be combined and treated as a spatially and temporally complete historical truth dataset.

## 5. Shallow-water solver diagnostics

The solver uses a depth-averaged nonlinear shallow-water finite-volume method with Rusanov fluxes, hydrostatic reconstruction, SSP-RK2 time integration, wind forcing, bottom friction, Coriolis acceleration and an eastern tidal boundary.

### 5.1 Mass-balance accounting

Across the 18 synthetic scenarios, relative mass-balance residuals were approximately 1.16e-16 to 2.09e-16.

This indicates that the tracked volume changes are consistent with the accumulated boundary exchange to machine precision.

It validates the accounting implementation for the tested scenarios. It does not independently establish that the bathymetry, boundary conditions, forcing or simulated ocean dynamics are physically accurate.

### 5.2 Wind sensitivity

Wind-versus-calm comparisons show a depth-dependent response.

The strongest domain-wide differences occur in the shallowest depth band:

| Depth band | RMS current-vector difference |
|---|---:|
| 0–10 m | 0.006772 m/s |
| 10–20 m | 0.000642 m/s |
| 20–50 m | 0.000432 m/s |
| 50–100 m | 0.000315 m/s |
| 100–200 m | 0.000254 m/s |
| 200–400 m | 0.000182 m/s |
| 400–700 m | 0.000141 m/s |

The strongest local response reaches approximately 0.0668 m/s in a coherent shallow coastal patch around hours five to six.

This concentration is consistent with depth-integrated wind forcing producing a stronger velocity response in shallower water. It is not, by itself, evidence of a numerical instability or a physically validated coastal current.

### 5.3 Wind-stress formulation

Inspection of the solver's conservative momentum equations found that wind forcing is applied in the form:

`rho_air * Cd * |U| * U / rho_water`

This is dimensionally consistent with a depth-integrated momentum tendency. The absence of an explicit division by water depth in this conservative formulation is not a demonstrated bug.

No wind-forcing correction is justified by the inspection performed.

### 5.4 Overall solver assessment

The diagnostics did not establish a specific solver defect that should be corrected before model evaluation.

They did identify limitations in the simplified forcing, domain data and initial conditions. A future solver-validation exercise should compare independent physical quantities and benchmark selected cases, rather than relying solely on internal conservation checks.

## 6. Independent validation readiness

BE57 found that independent real-world validation of FNO V2 is currently blocked.

Available observations do not provide the complete compatible historical fields required for a defensible spatial forecast evaluation.

The UHSLC gauge is useful for point water-level comparison but cannot validate spatial current predictions.

GLORYS daily means are useful for regional context but are not an independent hourly current-observation reference.

The Copernicus merged tide product is a candidate source for further water-level reconstruction, but datum and variable compatibility must be resolved.

A defensible evaluation requires:

1. Compatible historical initial water-level and current fields.
2. Historically appropriate wind forcing.
3. A compatible tidal boundary condition.
4. Verified vertical datum and spatial alignment.
5. Independent observations not used to construct or tune the evaluated forecast.
6. Metrics separated by variable, forecast lead time and relevant spatial region.
7. Comparison against persistence and appropriate physical or harmonic baselines.

Until these conditions are met, real-world forecast skill must be reported as unestablished.

## 7. Domain adaptation options

The following options are recommended in priority order.

### Priority 1 — Improve synthetic scenario coverage

Expand the training scenario distribution to include:

- Wider wind-speed ranges.
- More varied wind directions.
- Time-varying wind forcing where supported.
- Independently varied tidal amplitudes and phases.
- More representative tidal constituents and boundary behaviour.
- Varied, physically consistent initial water-level and current fields.

These changes address known limitations in the current synthetic training distribution.

### Priority 2 — Investigate bathymetric uncertainty

Before relying on shallow-water predictions:

- Identify authoritative local bathymetric survey sources.
- Compare GEBCO depths against independent observations where available.
- Preserve source-type metadata and uncertainty information.
- Investigate sensitivity of shallow-water simulations to plausible depth perturbations.
- Avoid interpreting the 250 m interpolated grid as independently measured 250 m bathymetry.

### Priority 3 — Reconstruct compatible historical conditions

Investigate whether hourly wind, tidal boundary and initial-state information can be assembled consistently for selected historical periods.

Do not treat daily GLORYS fields as hourly observations or mix sea-level variables with different physical meanings.

### Priority 4 — Evaluate adaptation methods

Once a defensible historical reconstruction and independent evaluation set exist, compare:

- Improved synthetic-data training.
- Normalization strategies appropriate to the combined data distribution.
- Fine-tuning using a strictly separated training period.
- Hybrid approaches combining learned predictions with physical constraints or model outputs.

Any adaptation experiment must be compared against the existing model and suitable baselines using untouched independent observations.

## 8. Risks and limitations

- No independent spatial current observations have been established for model validation.
- The available ERA5 sample covers only three days.
- Gauge observations are point measurements and do not provide spatial current fields.
- Absolute vertical datum compatibility remains unresolved.
- GLORYS temporal and spatial resolution limits direct hourly comparison.
- GEBCO source classifications are not quantitative uncertainty estimates.
- The solver's mass-balance consistency does not establish physical accuracy.
- Synthetic test metrics do not establish real-world forecast skill.
- No adaptation experiment has been evaluated against independent real-world observations.

The model must not be presented as suitable for navigation, vessel safety or operational marine decisions based on the current evidence.

## 9. Final recommendation

**Domain adaptation: justified as a research direction.**

**Immediate fine-tuning or retraining: not recommended.**

**Real-world forecast skill: not established.**

The strongest next steps are to improve synthetic forcing and initial-state coverage, investigate shallow-water bathymetric uncertainty, and establish a compatible historical reconstruction with independent evaluation observations.

The available evidence explains plausible sources of domain mismatch but does not establish which adaptation method will improve real-world forecast accuracy.

No solver changes, model retraining, checkpoint replacement or claims of improved real-world performance are made as part of this assessment.
